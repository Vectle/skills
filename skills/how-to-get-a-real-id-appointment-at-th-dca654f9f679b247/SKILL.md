Open your state driver licensing agency REAL ID page and read its own document checklist and visit rules before booking anything. States decide the exact documents, how recent address proofs must be, and whether visits are by appointment, same day queue, or walk in. Success check: you are working from your state checklist, not a list from another state.

Pick one identity and lawful status document from the federal list in 6 CFR 37.11: a valid unexpired US passport, a certified copy of a birth certificate filed with a state vital statistics office, a Consular Report of Birth Abroad, a valid Permanent Resident Card, an unexpired employment authorization document, an unexpired foreign passport with a valid US visa and approved I-94, or a naturalization or citizenship certificate. Success check: your document is an original or certified copy, unexpired where the rule requires it, and shows your full legal name and date of birth.

Line up Social Security proof: your Social Security card, or a W-2, an SSA-1099, a non SSA-1099, or a pay stub showing your name and full number. Note that California says the card itself is not required even though the number is. Success check: the document shows the full number, not just the last four digits.

Collect two proofs of the address of your principal residence, such as a utility bill, bank statement, lease, mortgage statement, or insurance document, printed if your state requires hard copies. Illinois, as one state example, requires hard copies, says phone images are not accepted, and dates bank statements within 90 days. Success check: both documents show your name and the same home street address, and a post office box is not your only address proof.

If your current name differs from your identity document, add the certified link for each change, such as a certified marriage certificate, a divorce decree showing the change, or a court order. Success check: someone reading only your folder can trace your name from the identity document to the name you use now.

Book or queue the way your state directs, then attend in person with the originals. In California, complete the application online, upload the identity and residency documents, save the confirmation code, make a DMV appointment, and bring the code and the original documents, which the DMV does not keep. Success check: you leave with the application accepted and a receipt or temporary document, not a list of missing papers.
