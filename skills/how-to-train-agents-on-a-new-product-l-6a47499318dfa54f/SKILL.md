## TL;DR
Support should never learn about a launch from a customer ticket. Get the docs two weeks early, build a launch FAQ from beta tickets and known edge cases, make every agent use the feature hands-on, and run a daily 15-minute huddle during launch week. The goal is simple: no agent faces a launch question they have not already seen.

## The query

```text
how to train agents on a new product launch
```

## Use this when

- A product or feature launch is coming
- Launch weeks overwhelm the queue
- Agents learn about releases from customers
- Beta feedback predicts support pain

## Not for

- General new-hire onboarding
- Sales team training
- Launch marketing
- Training on existing products

## Steps

### 1. Get docs and access two weeks early

Insist on draft docs, a staging account, and the known-issues list before launch. If product says docs are not ready, train from the beta tickets and the spec. "We will brief you at launch" means you will be briefed by angry customers.

Expected output: every agent with docs access and hands-on time before day one.

### 2. Build the launch FAQ from beta tickets

Beta users already asked the questions your customers will ask. Turn the top 20 into an FAQ with real answers, including the awkward ones product would rather not document. An FAQ written from beta pain beats one written from the press release.

Expected output: a 20-question FAQ grounded in real beta confusion.

### 3. Make everyone use the feature

No agent answers questions about a thing they have not touched. Assign hands-on exercises: complete the new workflow, hit the known edge cases, file a test ticket. Fifteen minutes of doing beats an hour of slides.

Expected output: agents who have personally completed the new workflow.

### 4. Run a daily huddle during launch week

Fifteen minutes, every morning: what new questions came in yesterday, what answers worked, what needs escalating to product. Update the FAQ live in the huddle. Launch week is when the FAQ earns its keep or goes stale.

Expected output: a living FAQ updated daily from real launch tickets.

### 5. Debrief after week two

What did the FAQ miss, which macros need writing, what should product fix versus document. Feed it all back: to the knowledge base, to the macro library, and to product as structured feedback. Then stand down the huddle.

Expected output: a debrief doc with actions for docs, macros, and product.

## Variant phrasings

### training customer support for product launch

The full five steps. Training is the pre-launch work; the huddle is the launch-week work.

### support readiness checklist for launch

Steps 1 through 3 as a checklist: docs in hand, FAQ built, hands-on done. If any box is unchecked, you are not ready.

### how to prepare support team for new feature release

Same five steps, smaller scale. A feature release gets a 10-question FAQ and a three-day huddle instead of the full program.

## Why it happens

Product teams launch; support teams absorb. The training gap exists because launches are planned around the release date, not the first customer question, which arrives within hours. Agents without prep do the worst thing: they guess, or they stall, and both destroy trust at the exact moment attention is highest. Two weeks of prep is cheap insurance against a launch week of chaos.

## Edge cases

- Surprise launch with no warning: skip to steps 3 and 4 immediately. Hands-on plus huddles beat waiting for docs that are not coming.
- The feature is buggy at launch: train agents on workarounds first, fixes second. Customers need a path forward today.
- Launch slips: keep the FAQ warm with a quick review each week. Stale launch prep is worse than none, because agents trust it.
- Tiny team, no time for huddles: a shared thread with the same three questions works. The ritual matters more than the meeting.
- Agents support multiple products: prioritize the launch FAQ over routine work for the first week. Everything else can wait three days.
