## TL;DR
Remote blameless reviews work when the timeline is written before the meeting, the facilitator protects the blameless norm explicitly, and the meeting ends with owned action items, not just discussion. The remote format actually helps: async timeline building, written input from quieter voices, and a recording for those who could not attend. Blameless is a facilitation discipline, not a vibe.

## The query
```text
how to run a blameless incident review remotely
```

## Use this when
- Reviews drift into blame, especially remote ones
- Distributed teams need a review format that works
- Reviews happen but change nothing
- Introducing blameless culture to a skeptical team

## Not for when
- The technical root-cause investigation (do that before)
- Real-time incident response
- Performance management (never mix with reviews)

## Steps

### Step 1: Build the timeline before the meeting
Collect a minute-by-minute timeline from logs, chat, and the responders' notes, and share it ahead of time. The meeting is for understanding decisions, not reconstructing what happened. A shared doc beats screen-sharing someone's memory.
Expected output: a timeline everyone has read before the call starts.

### Step 2: Set the blameless frame explicitly
Open by stating the rule: we assume everyone made reasonable decisions with the information they had; we are here to improve the system. Name it out loud every time, especially with new attendees. Remote silence makes blame harder to spot, so the explicit frame matters more.
Expected output: participants correcting toward systems language during the discussion.

### Step 3: Ask about decisions, not people
Structure questions around the system: what information was available at that moment, what made that action seem right, what would have helped. Never why did you do that. The facilitator redirects personal questions to system questions in real time.
Expected output: a discussion of contributing factors, not a list of mistakes by individuals.

### Step 4: Include async input
Let people add comments to the timeline doc before and after the meeting. Remote teams span timezones; the best insight often comes from someone who could not attend live. Read the async comments into the discussion.
Expected output: input from the full responder set, not just who made the call.

### Step 5: End with owned action items
Every review produces action items with an owner and a date, focused on systemic fixes. Review the previous review's action items first: a review that does not check last time's homework teaches everyone that reviews do not matter.
Expected output: 2-5 systemic action items, owned, dated, and tracked to completion.
