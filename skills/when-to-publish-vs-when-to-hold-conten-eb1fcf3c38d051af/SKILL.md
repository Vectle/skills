## TL;DR

Publish immediately when the content is evergreen or the value decays fast; hold when the piece only makes sense alongside a launch, announcement, or embargoed detail. The decision is about dependency, not quality: if the content needs the launch to exist, hold it, otherwise ship it. Staged drafts with a publish checklist beat both premature leaks and last-minute scrambles.

```text
when to publish vs when to hold content for launch timing
```

## Use this when

- You have content tied to a product launch, feature release, or announcement date
- Evergreen pieces are piling up in drafts while you wait for "the right moment"
- You need to coordinate a content spike with a launch window
- Embargoed or unreleased details appear in a draft

## Not for this skill when

- You are scheduling routine weekly content (a content calendar covers that)
- The content has no time sensitivity at all (just publish it)
- You need press/embargo logistics with journalists (different playbook)

## Steps

1. Classify each piece: launch-dependent or evergreen. Launch-dependent pieces reference the launch itself, unreleased features, or embargoed numbers. Expected: a clear label on every draft.

```
Labels: launch-dependent (needs the launch) vs evergreen (stands alone).
If in doubt, ask: does this sentence make sense without the launch?
```

2. Publish evergreen pieces immediately. They build the back catalog and SEO runway that the launch pieces will link into. Expected: the library grows while the launch cooks.

```
Rule: evergreen ships on its normal cadence, never waits for launch day.
```

3. Stage launch-dependent pieces as complete drafts. Fully written, formatted, tagged, canonical URLs set, just unpublished. Expected: launch day is a button press, not a writing sprint.

```
Staged draft checklist: title, body, tags, canonical URL,
publish target, publish date field filled in.
```

4. Build the launch-day publish order. Announcement first, then deep dives, then social posts pointing at both. Expected: a timed sequence instead of everything dropping at once.

```
Order: 1) announcement post, 2) technical deep dive,
3) social threads linking to both, 4) cross-posts to secondary channels.
```

5. Scrub held drafts for leaks. Check staged pieces for unreleased details that could surface early (previews, RSS feeds, sitemaps). Expected: nothing embargoed leaks before launch.

```
Checklist: no public preview URLs, RSS excludes staged drafts,
staging environments not indexed.
```

## Variant phrasings

### Should I hold content for launch day

Only the launch-dependent pieces. Holding evergreen content just delays its compounding.

### Content embargo strategy for agent launches

Stage everything as complete drafts, scrub for leaks, publish in a timed order on the day.

### Publishing cadence around a launch

Normal cadence for evergreen up to launch week, a deliberate pause of 1 to 2 days before launch so the announcement owns the window, then the staged sequence.

## Why it happens

Launches concentrate attention, so launch-dependent content gets more reach inside the window and less outside it, while evergreen content compounds the longer it is live. Holding evergreen pieces for launch day trades guaranteed compounding time for a one-day spike that the spike pieces already cover. The failure mode is a launch with no back catalog: new visitors find one announcement and nothing to read next.

## Edge cases / pitfalls

- A "soft launch" that drifts means held pieces rot; set a hard publish date for staged drafts.
- Leaks through RSS or staging previews are more common than deliberate ones; check the plumbing.
- Dont hold everything for launch week: an empty feed in the weeks before looks dead.
- If the launch slips, publish the non-sensitive staged pieces rather than letting them age.
- Announcement posts age fast; the deep dives carry the long-term value, so write them to survive the launch context.
