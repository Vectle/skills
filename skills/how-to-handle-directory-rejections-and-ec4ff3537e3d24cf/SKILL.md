## TL;DR

When a directory rejects your listing, read the reason, fix exactly what it names, wait a few weeks, and reapply once with the fix visible. Most rejections are about fit or completeness, both fixable. What never works is reapplying unchanged, arguing, or submitting again the next day.

```text
how to handle directory rejections and reapply
```

## Use this when

- A directory or registry rejected your listing
- An awesome-list PR was closed
- Deciding whether a rejection is worth another attempt

## Not for this skill when

- The rejection says the tool is out of scope (believe it, move on)
- You have already reapplied once (twice is the limit, usually once)
- The reason is a policy you cannot or will not meet

## Steps

1. Read the rejection reason twice. Separate "incomplete submission" and "out of scope" from "not right now". Expected: you can quote the reason back accurately.

2. Fix the named issue completely. Missing screenshots, wrong category, thin description: whatever it names, fix it properly, not cosmetically. Expected: a resubmission that visibly addresses the reason.

```
rejection log:
  directory: [name]
  reason: [quoted]
  fix: [what changed]
  reapply date: [3-4 weeks out]
```

3. Wait before reapplying. Three to four weeks is respectful; next-day resubmission reads as not having fixed anything. Expected: the gap is visible in your tracking sheet.

4. Reapply once, referencing the fix briefly. "Resubmitting with the requested screenshots added" is enough. Expected: a clean second attempt.

5. If rejected again, accept it. Log the directory as a no, and put the effort into directories that want you. Expected: no third attempt, no grudge.

## Variant phrasings

### directory listing rejected how to reapply

Fix the stated reason, wait three to four weeks, reapply once, accept the second verdict.

### my submission was rejected what now

Read the reason, fix it for real, and give it one more shot after a decent interval.

### reapplying after a directory rejection

One fix, one wait, one reapply. Then move on regardless.

## Why it happens

Maintainers reject quickly and often with terse reasons because review queues are long. A rejection is usually about the submission, not the tool: incomplete kits, wrong categories, scope mismatches. Those are fixable, which is why one careful reapply works and arguing never does.

## Edge cases / pitfalls

- "Out of scope" is final; no fix changes the scope.
- Paid-placement rejections mean the directory wants money; decide your policy, do not haggle.
- Keep the rejection reasons; patterns across directories reveal real positioning problems.
- Never have a second account resubmit after a rejection; maintainers notice.
