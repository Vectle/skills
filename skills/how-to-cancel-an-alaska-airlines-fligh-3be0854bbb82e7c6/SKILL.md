Look up the reservation on alaskaair.com or in the Alaska app and select cancel. Success check: the reservation is open and still before departure of the first flight.

On a Main or First Class refundable fare, complete the cancellation for a refund. Success check: the refund is headed to your original form of payment.

On a Main or First Class nonrefundable fare, choose where the credit goes: send it to My Wallet if you have an account, or take the credit certificate by email. Success check: the credit appears in your Wallet, usually almost immediately and within 7 days at the outside.

On a Saver fare, count the days to the first flight before you cancel. At 14 days or more, cancel and take the 50 percent credit. Inside 14 days, expect no credit and consider whether a change of plans is worth more than canceling. Success check: you know the credit percentage before you confirm, not after.

If Alaska changed your schedule by more than an hour or canceled the flight, take the refund to your original form of payment instead of a credit. Success check: the refund option is selected for the impacted flight.

## Community-reported tips (not Alaska Airlines's published policy)

The tips below come from traveler reports, frequent-flyer forums, and consumer guides, not from the airline. Treat them as street knowledge that can drift, and check the current policy before you rely on one.

- Saver's 14-day line is absolute in practice: cancel 14 or more days out and you get 50 percent back as Wallet credit; inside 14 days you get nothing, and Saver cannot be changed. Do the half-value math before cancelling a Saver fare to chase a price drop.
- The 24-hour window is a Saver ticket's only full-value exit, and Alaska's version is wider than the federal rule: any fare refunds to your original payment if you cancel within 24 hours of purchase and departure is more than 24 hours away. On a fare drop inside that window, book the cheaper ticket first, then cancel the original and take cash back, not credit.
- Wallet funds expire about 12 months from the original ticket issue, or 30 days from the cancellation, whichever is later, and it is a book-by date: the new trip can be up to about 11 months after it. Funds are spent soonest-expiring first, and only on alaskaair.com.
- Community-reported wallet reset, reaffirmed on FlyerTalk in late 2025 but contested in the same threads: deposit emailed vouchers into a Wallet, book a Main Cabin ticket (never Saver) for the full value dated before the old expiry, wait more than 24 hours, then cancel, and travelers report the value returns with a fresh one-year clock. Before confirming any cancellation, read the cancel screen: it states how much comes back and where. If it does not show a Wallet return, change the placeholder ticket to your real flight instead of cancelling it.
- Alaska ended post-purchase price adjustments in October 2026. Older advice to call and ask for the difference when the fare drops is stale; cancel and rebook is now the only fare-drop route.
- Bookings made through an agent carry a $30 per person charge since July 2026, so book and do simple changes online. For simple agent help, texting 82008 or using chat is repeatedly reported faster than the phone queue.
- A gift certificate does not expire, until you spend it: redeem one for a nonrefundable ticket and cancel more than 24 hours after purchase, and the value returns as a credit with a one-year clock. The non-expiry does not survive the round trip.
- Do not count on a goodwill or bereavement exception to rescue a Saver ticket. One detailed October 2026 firsthand case was refused on text and again after more than an hour on hold. Alaska's bereavement fare is 10 percent off a new ticket, not a waiver on an existing one.
