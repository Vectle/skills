## TL;DR

When impressions climb but clicks stay flat, Google is showing you more often and searchers are skipping you: that is a CTR problem, and the fix order is title first, meta description second, then checking what changed in the SERP around you. Dont chase more impressions until the click-through on the ones you have is healthy. Visibility without clicks is just free advertising for the result below you.

```text
impressions up clicks flat: diagnosing CTR problems
```

## Use this when

- GSC shows impressions rising while clicks stay flat over weeks
- A page ranks on page one but gets a below-average CTR for its position
- New pages get seen but not clicked
- You need to decide between snippet fixes and content fixes

## Not for this skill when

- Impressions are also flat or falling (that is a ranking or relevance problem)
- Clicks dropped suddenly (check for SERP feature changes or a site issue first)
- The page doesnt rank in the top 20 (nobody sees the snippet)

## Steps

1. Confirm the pattern per query, not site-wide. Filter GSC to the queries driving the impression growth and check their CTR individually. Expected: a list of specific queries with high impressions and low CTR.

```
Segment: queries sorted by impressions, CTR column visible.
Pattern confirmed when top-impression queries sit under 2 percent CTR.
```

2. Inspect the live SERP for those queries. Search each query and look at your snippet versus the neighbors: who has the clearer title, the richer result, the brand the searcher trusts. Expected: you see exactly why a searcher skips you.

```
Checklist: your title vs theirs, rich results present,
brand recognition gap, SERP features pushing you down.
```

3. Rewrite the title to win the glance test. Lead with the query's key terms and a clear promise. Expected: the snippet visibly matches the searcher's intent better than before.

```
Rule: query terms up front, specific promise, under 60 chars.
One page, one rewrite, then measure.
```

4. Rewrite the meta description as the closer. Deliverable plus outcome in under 155 characters. Expected: the two-line pitch that turns a glance into a click.

```
Formula: what you get + the outcome, plain words.
```

5. Measure for 2 to 4 weeks, then iterate. CTR needs a few thousand impressions to be trustworthy. Expected: a verdict per query (fixed, improved, still broken) and a next step.

```
Measure: CTR per query, 2 to 4 weeks after the change.
Still flat: the problem may be SERP competition or intent mismatch, not the snippet.
```

## Variant phrasings

### Why am I getting impressions but no clicks

Your snippet loses the glance test; fix the title first, then the description, then check the SERP around you.

### CTR drop with stable rankings

Rankings held but clicks fell usually means a SERP feature or competitor snippet change; inspect the live SERP.

### How to improve SERP click through rate

Title mirrors the query, description pitches the outcome, and the page delivers on both.

## Why it happens

Impressions measure Google's willingness to show you; clicks measure searchers' willingness to choose you. Those are different judgments. Impression growth with flat clicks means Google's relevance model likes the page but human scanners dont, which almost always comes down to the snippet: the title doesnt contain their words, the description doesnt promise their outcome, or a competitor's rich result looks more authoritative. More impressions just multiplies a broken conversion rate.

## Edge cases / pitfalls

- Some queries are inherently low-CTR (instant answers, navigational); benchmark against the query type, not a universal number.
- A new SERP feature (AI overview, video pack) can absorb clicks without your snippet changing; check the SERP before rewriting.
- Dont rewrite titles weekly; CTR data needs weeks to stabilize, and churn confuses the measurement.
- Brand vs non-brand matters: branded queries convert at multiples of non-brand; segment them.
- If the page doesnt deliver what the new snippet promises, bounce rates will punish you; the snippet is a promise the page must keep.
