## TL;DR
Finance decides fast when the packet is complete: the disputed charges with dates and amounts, the contract or plan terms, the customer's payment history, and your recommendation. Incomplete packets bounce, and every bounce adds days. Build the packet once as a template, fill it per dispute, and include a recommendation. Finance wants a decision to ratify, not a mystery to solve.

## The query

```text
how to escalate a billing dispute to finance
```

## Use this when

- A customer disputes a charge support cannot resolve
- Refund requests above your approval limit
- Finance keeps bouncing disputes back for more info
- Building the billing escalation process

## Not for

- Setting refund or billing policy
- Fraud investigations
- Collections or dunning
- Pricing negotiations

## Steps

### 1. Assemble the evidence packet

Disputed charges (dates, amounts, invoice numbers), the plan or contract terms in effect, the customer's payment and dispute history, and the full ticket thread. Five items. If any is missing, say why.

Expected output: a complete packet, no gaps unexplained.

### 2. State your recommendation with reasoning

"Recommend full refund of [amount]: the customer was charged after canceling, and the cancellation record confirms it." Finance decides faster when support takes a position. "Please advise" is how packets sit for a week.

Expected output: a one-paragraph recommendation.

### 3. Name the customer impact and the clock

"This is an enterprise account up for renewal in 30 days" or "The customer has disputed with their bank; we have 10 days to respond." Impact and deadline set the priority honestly.

Expected output: impact and deadline on the packet.

### 4. Send it to the right person with the right subject

Know who in finance owns disputes. Subject line: "Billing dispute: [customer], [amount], decision needed by [date]." Route to a person, not a mailing list.

Expected output: the packet with a named owner and a date.

### 5. Tell the customer what happens next and when

"We have escalated this to our finance team; you will hear from me by [date]." Then follow up with finance before that date. The customer should never chase you for the escalation you promised.

Expected output: the customer informed with a date you keep.

## Template: the escalation packet

```text
To: [finance owner]  Subject: Billing dispute: [customer], [amount], decision by [date]

Customer: [name], [tier], [tenure]
Dispute: [one line]
Charges: [invoice numbers, dates, amounts]
Terms: [plan/contract terms in effect, with link]
History: [payment record, prior disputes]
Recommendation: [refund/credit/deny] because [reasoning]
Customer impact: [renewal/churn/bank dispute]
Needed by: [date]

Ticket: [link]
```

## Variant phrasings

### billing escalation template

Steps 1 and 2 plus the template. Packet plus recommendation.

### finance approval for refund

Steps 2 through 4. Recommendation, impact, right person.

### customer disputing charge over my limit

Full sequence. The packet is the whole game.

## Why it works

Finance teams are not slow; they are starved of context. A complete packet with a recommendation turns their job from investigation into decision, which is a 10-minute task instead of a 3-day one. The recommendation matters: it shows support did the work and gives finance something concrete to agree with or adjust.

## Edge cases

- Bank dispute already filed: lead with the deadline. Bank windows do not wait for internal process.
- The contract terms are ambiguous: say so and recommend the customer-friendly reading. Ambiguity you wrote is not the customer's problem.
- Repeat disputes from the same customer: flag the pattern. Finance may want a different resolution than another refund.
- Finance denies: communicate the denial with the reasoning, plus what you can offer instead. Never forward a bare "no."
