## TL;DR

Reply when you can add information, correct a fact about your work, or answer a direct question; stay quiet when the thread is heated, the topic is outside your competence, or your reply would read as self-promotion. The default for an agent should be to listen more than it talks: a reply is a bid for attention, so spend it where you have something real to add. When in doubt, sit it out.

```text
when to reply vs when to stay quiet on social
```

## Use this when

- An agent is unsure whether to join a thread or conversation
- You need a policy for agent replies across venues
- Replies have backfired before and you want rules
- You are briefing an agent on social judgment

## Not for this skill when

- You need crisis communication (different playbook, operator-led)
- The reply is a customer support response (answer it; that is the job)
- You are planning proactive posts (different skill)

## Steps

1. Check: do I add information? If your reply contributes a fact, a data point, or an experience nobody has shared, reply. Expected: replies that move the thread forward.

```
Reply when: new information, a relevant counterexample,
an answer from direct experience.
```

2. Check: is it my topic? If the thread is about your agent's actual work or a question directed at you, engage. Expected: presence where you belong, absence where you dont.

```
Reply when: asked directly, or the topic is your operational domain.
Sit out: adjacent topics where you would be guessing.
```

3. Check: is the thread heated? If tempers are up, any agent reply reads as provocative regardless of content. Expected: zero replies that escalate.

```
Sit out: pile-ons, callouts, ideological fights.
Exception: a factual correction about your own work, posted once, calmly.
```

4. Check: would this read as self-promotion? If the honest answer is yes, dont reply, or reply without mentioning your project. Expected: no replies that smell like marketing.

```
Test: remove your project mention; is the reply still worth posting?
If no, it was promotion, not contribution.
```

5. Default to quiet on ties. If two checks say reply and two say sit out, sit out. Expected: a bias toward restraint that protects reputation.

```
Tie-break rule: when in doubt, stay quiet.
A missed reply costs nothing; a bad reply costs trust.
```

## Variant phrasings

### Should an agent reply to this thread

Run the four checks: adds information, your topic, not heated, not self-promotion.

### Social media reply etiquette for bots

Contribute or stay quiet; never argue, never promote, never pile on.

### When to engage vs ignore online criticism

Engage once with facts on your own work; ignore tone; sit out heated threads.

## Why it happens

Every reply is a claim on strangers' attention, and agents face a higher bar because readers start skeptical: a human's casual reply is fine, an agent's identical reply gets scrutinized for motive. Restraint is therefore strategic, not timid: selective replies build a reputation for substance, while frequent low-value replies train the community to ignore you. The agents with the best standing are the ones whose replies are rare enough to be read carefully.

## Edge cases / pitfalls

- Silence on a direct question looks evasive; direct questions deserve answers.
- "Adding information" includes correcting misinformation about your work; do it once, with evidence.
- Dont reply to every mention; mentions in heated threads are bait, not invitations.
- If the operator wouldnt say it, the agent shouldnt post it; the reply policy inherits the operator's judgment.
- Review reply patterns monthly; drift toward chattiness is the usual failure mode.
