Open My Trips on flyfrontier.com, pull up the booking, and identify what you bought: Basic or Standard fare, Economy fare, or a bundle. Success check: you know your fare type before opening the cancel flow.

If you are within 24 hours of booking a flight that was 7 or more days away, cancel now. Success check: the refund goes to your original form of payment instead of a credit.

On a Basic or Standard fare, cancel before departure and accept the math: the $149 fee per passenger, per direction, plus certain carrier charges, comes off, and the rest is retained as a travel credit. Success check: the cancel screen shows the fee and the remaining credit before you confirm.

On an Economy fare or a bundle, cancel before departure with a $0 cancellation fee. Success check: the full remaining value is retained, as a credit or back to your payment method if you bought a refundable bundle fare.

Save the travel credit and its terms. Success check: you have the credit details recorded before you close the page.

## Community-reported tips (not Frontier Airlines's published policy)

The tips below come from traveler reports, frequent-flyer forums, and consumer guides, not from the airline. Treat them as street knowledge that can drift, and check the current policy before you rely on one.

- The Works bundle is a legacy product you generally cannot buy anymore. Frontier's current bundles (Economy, Premium, Business, First) cancel with no fee, but the full value comes back as travel credit, not cash, per Frontier's November 2026 FAQ. The Works refund route only applies to legacy purchases: cancel first, then separately submit the online Refund Request Form and choose refund over credit; it is not automatic.
- Learn the three instruments before you cancel, because they run on three different clocks: a voluntary travel credit lasts 12 months; a goodwill or bump voucher lasts 90 days and cannot be extended; a credit chosen instead of an owed refund for a major delay or cancellation lasts 5 years. Read the label and expiry date on your own email rather than trusting the word credit in a forum post.
- A schedule change of 1 to 3 hours buys you a credit, not a refund. Over 3 hours domestic or 6 hours international, or a Frontier cancellation, buys a cash refund if you do not travel and do not accept a credit instead.
- If Frontier caused the cancellation and you want cash, do not accept the credit that is offered first. Advocacy cases show credit is the default offer, and refunds in practice often run past the published 7 business days.
- Cancel before departure, whatever else you do. A no-show forfeits the remaining value entirely; cancelling preserves it as credit minus any Basic fee.
- Start with live chat, WhatsApp, or the online form, not the phone. A November 2026 firsthand report had both published phone numbers unanswered, with Frontier's own message steering to chat, though chat also took hours in that account.
- Expired Frontier credit is dead in practice: Frontier refused to reinstate one even where it had waived the cancellation fee as goodwill (BBB case, September 2025). And do not act on a verbal waiver promise, such as send a death certificate and we will refund you; a documented case had that promise refused after the paperwork was sent. Get any waiver in writing before you cancel.
