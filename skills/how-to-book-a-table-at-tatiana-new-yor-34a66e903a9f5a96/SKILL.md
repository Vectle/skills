Count back 28 days from the date you want and put the noon Eastern drop on your calendar. Success check: you know your exact drop day and time, and it falls on a day the restaurant is open.

Before noon, sign in to Resy, complete your profile, and save a payment card, since in demand bookings move too fast for checkout delays. Success check: you are signed in on the Tatiana venue page with your party size set before 12 p.m.

At noon, refresh the venue page and book the first acceptable time that appears, starting with the 5 to 7 p.m. slots if you want prime time. Success check: you get a Resy confirmation, not just a time showing as available.

If everything is gone, set a Resy Notify for your date with the widest time window you would accept, and turn on push notifications. Success check: the Notify shows as set for Tatiana on that date.

Act the second a Notify alert arrives, since the restaurants Notify list can run from about 800 to more than 2,000 people on a day and the table is not held for you. Success check: an alert converts into a confirmed booking in your Resy account.

If Resy stays empty, call the restaurant for help with a reservation, since its team says a live reservationist can sometimes find or squeeze in a table. Success check: you have spoken to the restaurant and know whether anything can be done for your date.

As a last route, walk in: the bar and the outdoor patio are held for walk ins, with the full menu served, and the restaurant recommends arriving right at 5 p.m. opening. Success check: your name is on the door or bar waitlist, which the restaurant says typically runs 45 minutes to an hour, and up to about two hours at the bar.
