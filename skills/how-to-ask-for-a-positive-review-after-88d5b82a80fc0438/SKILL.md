## TL;DR
Ask for the review right after a clear win, make it one tap, and ask for the story, not the stars. "Would you share what happened?" beats "Please leave five stars." Never ask after a mediocre interaction, never offer incentives, and never let the ask sound like a quota. Reviews earned from great support write themselves; your job is just to make it easy.

## The query

```text
how to ask for a positive review after support
```

## Use this when

- Building review generation into support workflows
- Review volume is low despite good service
- Training agents on the ask
- Reviews skew negative (silent happy customers)

## Not for

- Offering incentives for reviews
- Filtering or gating negative reviews
- Asking after bad or mediocre experiences
- Fake or solicited review schemes

## Steps

### 1. Ask only after clear wins

The customer said thank you unprompted, the problem is fully solved, the tone is warm. If you have to wonder whether it went well, it did not go well enough. Ask.

Expected output: asks only on genuinely great interactions.

### 2. Make it one tap

Link directly to the review page with the stars or form open. Every extra click halves completion. Prefill nothing, but remove all navigation.

Expected output: a direct link, no hunting.

### 3. Ask for the story, not the stars

"Would you mind sharing what happened? It helps other customers." Stories produce detailed, credible reviews. "Please rate us five stars" produces awkward compliance or nothing.

Expected output: story-framed asks.

### 4. Keep it separate from the survey

The CSAT survey measures you; the review helps future customers. Do not chain them in one breath. Let the survey close, then make the review ask its own small moment.

Expected output: two separate touches, not one combined ask.

### 5. Thank reviewers and read the reviews

Reply to reviews, especially detailed ones. And read them monthly: review text is unfiltered voice-of-customer about what great support looks like at your company.

Expected output: review responses and a monthly read-through.

## Template: the ask

```text
[Name], I am really glad we got this sorted.

If you have 30 seconds, would you share what happened in a quick review? It helps other customers know what to expect, and honest detail beats stars. Here is the link: [direct link]

Either way, thanks for your patience today. - [Agent]
```

## Variant phrasings

### request review after good support

Steps 1 through 3. Win, one tap, story.

### customer left a great review, now what

Step 5. Thank them, respond publicly, mine it for training.

### getting more google reviews from support

Steps 2 and 4. Direct link, separate from the survey.

## Why it works

Happy customers are silent; unhappy ones are motivated. The ask corrects that asymmetry, but only when it is honest: asked after real wins, framed around helping others, with zero pressure. Incentivized or pressured reviews read as fake and train customers to distrust the rating.

## Edge cases

- The customer offers to review unprompted: say yes fast and send the link. Do not be coy.
- B2B customers: ask for a quote or case study instead of a public review; procurement often forbids public reviews.
- The interaction was fine but not great: do not ask. "Fine" reviews dilute the great ones.
- Negative review arrives anyway: respond publicly, own it, take it private. Never argue in the review thread.
