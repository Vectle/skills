Find your release morning: take your arrival date, go back five months, and use the 15th of that month. Arriving May 20 means booking on December 15 at 7 am Pacific. Success check: you have one exact calendar date and time, not a range.

Confirm your campground uses the five-month block: Upper Pines, Lower Pines, North Pines, Wawona, and Hodgdon Meadow do. Half of Tuolumne Meadows releases two months ahead on the 15th at 7 am Pacific instead, and most other campgrounds use shorter rolling windows. The campground's own page on Recreation.gov states its window.

Create a Recreation.gov account and save your payment details before release day. Success check: you can log in and reach the campground page without a password or card step left.

Log in by about 6:45 am Pacific on the 15th, open your campground page, and enter your arrival and departure dates so only the search is left to click.

At 7:00 am Pacific sharp, search and book the first site that fits your dates and vehicle length. Expect peak summer dates to be gone within minutes; do not spend the first minutes comparing individual sites.

If the block sells out, keep checking back over the following weeks: cancelled reservations return to inventory at random times, and early-morning checks are the standard second chance.

## Community-reported tips (not the agency's published rules)

The tips below come from trip reports, hiking forums, and permit guides, not from the agency. Treat them as street knowledge that can drift.

- If everything shows gone at 7:00, keep refreshing for the next 20 minutes or so. A site sits in another person's cart for 15 minutes while they check out, and sites that never get paid for drop back into inventory starting around 7:15, so release morning has a second wave.
- Tuesday to Thursday arrival dates are noticeably easier than weekends, and shoulder season dates in May and September are easier still; campground guides that track these releases report the same pattern every year.
- Cancellations bunch up in the last two weeks before an arrival date, as trips firm up and cancellation deadlines approach. Checking early in the morning during that window is the most productive second chance.
- Plenty of campers run a cancellation scanner instead of refreshing by hand; Campnab and Campflare are the services named most often, and they text you the moment a matching site opens.
