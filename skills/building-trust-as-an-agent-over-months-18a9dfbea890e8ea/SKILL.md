## TL;DR
Trust compounds from boring consistency: show up regularly, be right more often than not, disclose honestly, and fix mistakes visibly. There is no shortcut; a month of steady helpful replies buys what no launch-day blitz can. Track trust like a metric (thanks, solutions, mod relationships), review it quarterly, and let the ledger guide where you invest next.

```text
building trust as an agent over months not days
```

## Use this when
- You are planning a community presence that lasts quarters
- An agent is technically fine but nobody trusts it yet
- You need to justify slow community investment to a stakeholder
- You are diagnosing why mentions get ignored or flagged
- You are writing the long-term section of a participation policy

## Not for this skill when
- You need trust by next week (it does not work that way; say so)
- The venue is hostile to agents regardless of behavior (leave cleanly)
- You are handling an active trust incident (see the accusation skill)
- The question is brand trust with buyers, not community trust with members

## Steps
1. Commit to a cadence you can sustain for a year. Two thoughtful replies a week for twelve months beats fifty replies in January and silence after. Trust is a function of time present, and presence cannot be backfilled.
   ```text
   cadence: [n] quality replies per week, owner: [name], horizon: 12 months
   ```
   Expected output: a written commitment with an owner. Success check: month three looks like month one in the activity log.

2. Keep a reputation ledger per venue. Thanks received, solutions marked, corrections taken, moderator interactions, all dated. What gets measured gets sustained; the ledger also settles every "is this working" debate with evidence.
   ```text
   venue: [name]
   [date] reply thanked: [link]
   [date] marked as solution: [link]
   [date] correction accepted: [link]
   ```
   Expected output: a running ledger. Success check: you can chart trust signals per venue over time.

3. Be right, and be visibly careful when unsure. Trust in a community is mostly "this account's answers work". Qualify uncertainty openly ("I have not tested this on v3, but..."), and correct your own mistakes fast and publicly. One visible correction builds more trust than ten safe replies.
   ```text
   [ ] uncertain claims qualified in the reply itself
   [ ] own mistakes corrected within 24h, publicly
   ```
   Expected output: a record of qualified claims and fast corrections. Success check: regulars cite your replies to others.

4. Invest in relationships, not just replies. Thank people whose answers taught you, follow up on threads you joined, remember regulars' contexts. Communities are social; the agent that acts like a neighbor gets neighbor-level trust.
   ```text
   [ ] followed up on [n] threads this month
   [ ] thanked [n] contributors publicly
   ```
   Expected output: social evidence in the ledger. Success check: regulars greet your account by name (or handle).

5. Review quarterly and double down where trust is compounding. Venues where the ledger grows get more cadence; venues where it flatlines despite good behavior get a frank reassessment. Trust investment follows evidence like any investment.
   ```text
   quarterly: venue [name], ledger trend [up/flat/down], decision [invest/hold/exit]
   ```
   Expected output: dated invest/hold/exit decisions. Success check: cadence actually moved toward the compounding venues.

## Variant phrasings
### How long does it take to build reputation in a forum
Timeline phrasing. Months, not days; step 1's year-long cadence is the honest answer.

### Trust-building strategies for bots
Bot phrasing. Same playbook with extra emphasis on disclosure and visible corrections, since bots start with a trust deficit.

### Community reputation as a growth channel
Growth phrasing. Steps 2 and 5: the ledger makes reputation measurable, and measurement makes it investable.

### Why nobody trusts our agent account yet
Diagnostic phrasing. Check cadence consistency (step 1), correction record (step 3), and account age; the answer is usually "not enough months yet".

## Why it happens
Trust is a prediction: members predict your future replies from your past ones. Predictions need samples, and samples need time; no burst of activity compresses the calendar because the prediction being tested is "will they still be good in six months". Consistency, visible corrections, and social presence are the samples the prediction runs on. This is why shortcuts fail: they produce samples of intensity, not of duration.

## Edge cases / pitfalls
- A single bad month can erase a year. One spammy burst or one dishonest stretch resets the prediction; the ledger's job is to make that visible before it happens.
- Trust does not transfer between venues. A beloved account in one forum starts at zero in the next; budget the months per venue.
- Do not buy trust signals. Thank-trading or solution-begging corrupts the ledger and reads as manipulation when discovered.
- Staff changes reset your side too. If the owner in step 1 leaves, hand the cadence to a named successor the same week; gaps longer than a month restart the clock.
