## TL;DR

When a forum deletes your account for spam, first confirm it is really a deletion and not a login problem, then decide if the venue is worth one polite appeal. Most of the time the right move is to accept it, log what pattern got you flagged, and either restart properly or drop the venue. Never spin up a replacement account the same day; that is exactly what spammers do.

```text
what to do when a forum deletes your account for spam
```

## Use this when

- Your forum account vanished and you suspect a spam deletion
- You got a spam-deletion notice and need next steps
- You are deciding whether to appeal or move on

## Not for this skill when

- The account was flagged but still exists (use the unflag skill)
- You were actually spamming and know it
- You want to evade the deletion with new accounts

## Steps

1. Confirm the deletion. Try logging in, try password reset, check whether your profile page 404s. Expected: you know it is a deletion, not a locked password or a flag.
2. Read the notice if there is one. Deletion emails usually name the reason; save it. Expected: you know the stated cause.
3. Decide if the venue is worth an appeal. High-value venue with a real false positive: appeal once. Low-value venue or you were pushing it: move on. Expected: a decision, not a reflex.
4. If appealing, write one message. Short, factual, own what your posting pattern looked like, ask if there is a path back. Expected: sent, then you wait; one message, not a campaign.
5. Log the pattern. What did the account do in its last days: link density, post frequency, new threads vs replies. Expected: a note that stops the next account from repeating it.
6. Cool off before any restart. Wait at least a few days, use a fresh email, and start with link-free participation. Expected: a new account that doesnt trip the same filters.

## Variant phrasings

### forum account removed for spamming what to do

Same steps: confirm, decide on appeal, log the pattern, cool off.

### my account was deleted as spam but I wasnt spamming

Focus step 4 on the false-positive evidence: your actual posts were legitimate answers.

### banned for spam on a forum appeal process

Check whether the forum distinguishes bans from deletions; bans sometimes have a formal appeal form, deletions often dont.

## Why it happens

Spam deletions are usually the end of an automated chain: flags accumulate, a moderator or the system confirms, the account goes. By the time it is deleted, the pattern looked unambiguous from the outside, which is why the log in step 5 matters more than the appeal.

## Edge cases

- Some forums delete on first offense with no appeal path; accept it faster there.
- If the deletion email mentions IP or network-level blocking, a new account on the same setup will die the same way.
- Appeals that argue or quote policy at moderators almost never work; keep it humble and short.
- If several venues delete you in the same week, the problem is your playbook, not the venues; pause and review.
