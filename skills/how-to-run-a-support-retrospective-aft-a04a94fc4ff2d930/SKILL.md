## TL;DR
A support retro after a bad week needs one thing the week did not have: structure. Run a 45-minute session with three questions (what hurt, why it hurt, what changes), enforce blameless ground rules, and leave with at most three owned action items. The retro fails if it becomes a venting session with no owners, or a blame session that makes agents hide problems next time.

## The query

```text
how to run a support retrospective after a bad week
```

## Use this when

- The team just survived a brutal week of tickets
- Morale is low and people need the week to mean something
- You want a retro format for recurring rough patches
- Bad weeks keep repeating with no learning

## Not for

- Single-incident postmortems
- Individual performance reviews
- Quarterly planning or OKRs
- Customer-facing apologies

## Steps

### 1. Pull the data before the meeting

Ticket volume, top drivers, CSAT dip, SLA misses, handle-time spikes. Ten minutes of data keeps the retro honest and stops it from being driven by whoever is loudest.

Expected output: one page of numbers covering the bad week.

### 2. Set the ground rules out loud

Blameless: we talk about systems and processes, not people. No naming, no shaming. What is said in the retro stays in the retro. Say it at the start, every time.

Expected output: everyone in the room hears the rules before the first story.

### 3. Collect what hurt, silently first

Five minutes of silent writing on stickies or a doc: the moments that hurt most this week. Silent first, so the quiet agents get equal weight.

Expected output: a wall of specific painful moments, not general complaints.

### 4. Group and vote on the real drivers

Cluster the stickies into themes, then each person gets three votes. The top two or three themes are the retro's scope. Everything else goes on a parking list.

Expected output: two or three named themes the room agrees caused the pain.

### 5. Ask why five times per theme

For each theme, dig past the symptom. "Tickets spiked" becomes "the release shipped without a support heads-up" becomes "there is no release-to-support handoff." The fix lives at the root, not the symptom.

Expected output: a root cause per theme that a process change could fix.

### 6. Leave with at most three owned action items

Each action item has an owner and a date. Three is the max, more than that means nothing gets done. Assign, dont volunteer.

Expected output: three or fewer actions, each with a name and a deadline.

## 45-minute agenda

```text
0:00  Ground rules + data (facilitator, 5 min)
0:05  Silent writing: what hurt (everyone, 5 min)
0:10  Group into themes (facilitator, 5 min)
0:15  Vote on top themes (everyone, 5 min)
0:20  Why-five-times per theme (room, 15 min)
0:35  Action items: owners + dates (facilitator, 10 min)
```

## Variant phrasings

### support team retrospective format

The agenda above. Works monthly as a standing retro too, not just after bad weeks.

### how to run a blameless retrospective for support

Steps 2 and 5 are the blameless core. If anyone names a person, the facilitator redirects to the process immediately and visibly.

### what to do after a terrible week in customer support

This retro, plus one human thing: acknowledge the week was hard before the data slide. People cant do root-cause analysis while they are still raw.

## Why it happens

Bad weeks feel random, which makes them feel hopeless. The retro's real job is to show the week had causes, and causes can be fixed. Teams that retro after bad weeks stop having the same bad week twice. Teams that dont retro have the same bad week every month and call it "just how support is."

## Edge cases

- The bad week was one person's fault: still blameless. Ask what process let one person become the single point of failure.
- Remote or async teams: run the silent writing and voting in a doc over 24 hours, then a 30-minute call for the why-five-times and actions.
- Nothing actionable comes out: that usually means the digging stopped at symptoms. Run step 5 again on the top theme with a stricter facilitator.
- Follow-through dies: review last retro's action items in the first 5 minutes of the next one. Unfinished actions get named, not shamed.
