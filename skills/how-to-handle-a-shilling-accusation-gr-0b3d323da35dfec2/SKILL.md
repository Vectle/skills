## TL;DR
When accused of shilling, do not argue the accusation; acknowledge the concern, remove or edit the offending post, and thank the person for flagging it. Arguing converts a misunderstanding into a reputation event. One graceful response ends most accusations; the log entry it produces prevents the next one.

```text
how to handle a shilling accusation gracefully
```

## Use this when
- Someone publicly accuses you or your agent of shilling
- A moderator warns you about promotional tone
- A reply of yours gets called an ad in the comments
- You are briefing agents on what to do when accused
- You are reviewing an accusation to decide if it was fair

## Not for this skill when
- You are appealing a formal ban (different skill, different venue)
- The accusation is part of legal action (get a lawyer, not a skill)
- You actually were shilling and want to argue it was fine (it was not; fix it)
- The venue is one you have already decided to leave (leave cleanly instead)

## Steps
1. Pause and do not reply for at least an hour. Accusations trigger defensiveness, and defensive replies read as guilt even when you are innocent. The hour is not about strategy; it is about not writing the reply you will regret.
   ```text
   accusation seen: [time], reply scheduled no earlier than: [time + 1h]
   ```
   Expected output: a deliberate pause. Success check: the eventual reply contains zero sarcasm and zero counter-accusations.

2. Read the accusation as a moderator would, looking for the fair point. Even unfair accusations usually point at something real: a mention that was borderline, a ratio drifting promotional, a disclosure that was hard to find. Find the fair point before you respond.
   ```text
   fair point found: [one sentence]
   ```
   Expected output: one sentence of honest self-critique. Success check: you can state the accuser's case in a way they would agree with.

3. Reply briefly: acknowledge, fix, thank. No essays, no evidence dumps, no debating the definition of shilling. "Fair point, I have removed the link and will keep mentions to where they are directly asked for. Thanks for flagging it." That is the whole reply.
   ```text
   template: "Fair point. I have [removed/edited] the post. Thanks for flagging it; I will [specific change]."
   ```
   Expected output: a short public reply. Success check: under 50 words, no argument, no excuses.

4. Fix the underlying pattern, not just the post. If the accusation was fair, adjust the ratio, the relevance bar, or the disclosure; if it was unfair but pointed at a real pattern, adjust anyway. The reply handles the moment; the fix handles the future.
   ```text
   pattern change: [what changed in your participation rules]
   ```
   Expected output: a rule change or a re-briefed agent. Success check: the same accusation could not fairly recur next month.

5. Log the accusation and move on. Venue, thread, what was said, what you changed. Do not revisit the thread to check whether people forgave you; that reads as reputation management, which restarts the cycle.
   ```text
   logged: [venue, date, summary, change made]
   ```
   Expected output: a log entry. Success check: you never post in that thread about the accusation again.

## Variant phrasings
### Accused of shilling on Reddit, what to do
Venue-specific phrasing. Same playbook; note that public pile-ons escalate fast, so the one-hour pause in step 1 matters more.

### How to respond when called a shill
Response phrasing. Step 3 is the answer: acknowledge, fix, thank, under 50 words.

### Shilling accusation false positive
Fairness phrasing. Steps 2 and 4: find the fair point anyway and fix the pattern; being right but defensive still loses.

### Reputation repair after a spam flag
Repair phrasing. The repair is the pattern change in step 4 sustained over months, not any single reply.

## Why it happens
Accusations are pattern-matching by people who have been burned before; they are often wrong about you and right about the pattern they have seen a hundred times. Arguing asks them to update their model of you from one reply, which never works; fixing the post and the pattern gives them new evidence, which does. Grace works because it is the only response that cannot be screenshotted against you.

## Edge cases / pitfalls
- Do not delete the accusation thread or mass-delete your history. Deletion reads as a cover-up and escalates; edit or remove only the specific offending post.
- Private apologies to moderators plus unchanged public behavior fool nobody. The pattern change is the apology that counts.
- If the same venue produces repeated accusations despite real changes, the venue may just not want agents. Leaving cleanly beats winning an argument.
- Never sic your community on the accuser. Even a mild "my followers disagree" turns a misunderstanding into a war you will be remembered for.
