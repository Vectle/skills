## TL;DR
When asked to leave, leave promptly and completely: thank the moderator, stop posting that day, clean up your presence, and confirm it is done. Do not negotiate, do not post a farewell essay, and do not linger to "wrap up". A clean exit preserves the relationship and the option to return; a messy one becomes the story people tell about agents.

```text
how to leave a community cleanly when asked
```

## Use this when
- A moderator asks you or your agent to leave
- A venue's policy changed to exclude agents
- You have decided a venue is not a fit after all
- You are winding down participation across several venues
- You are briefing agents on exit behavior

## Not for this skill when
- You want to appeal the removal (different skill; appeals are rare and specific)
- The "ask" is ambiguous rather than explicit (clarify first)
- You are leaving because of a shilling accusation mid-dispute (handle the accusation first)
- The question is about deleting your own account for privacy reasons

## Steps
1. Acknowledge promptly and without argument. "Understood, I will wind down today. Thank you for having me." One reply, no debate about the fairness, no request for exceptions. The acknowledgement is the whole negotiation.
   ```text
   reply: "Understood. Winding down today, thank you."
   ```
   Expected output: a short public or mod-channel acknowledgement. Success check: under 30 words, zero argument.

2. Stop all posting the same day. Cancel scheduled posts, pause the agent's venue config, and tell anyone who might post on your behalf. A farewell tour of "last replies" is still posting after being asked to leave.
   ```text
   [ ] scheduled posts cancelled
   [ ] agent venue config paused
   [ ] no new replies after [date/time]
   ```
   Expected output: a silent account from the ack onward. Success check: the post history shows a clean stop.

3. Clean up the presence you leave behind. Update or remove the profile's promotional bits, leave genuinely helpful replies in place (deleting good answers punishes the community), and remove anything that was purely promotional. Ask the mods if they want anything specific removed.
   ```text
   [ ] profile neutralized
   [ ] promotional posts removed or edited
   [ ] helpful replies left intact
   [ ] mods asked: anything else to clean up? [answer]
   ```
   Expected output: a presence the mods are comfortable leaving up. Success check: the mod team confirms nothing further is needed.

4. Confirm completion briefly and then go quiet. One message: "Done, thanks again." Then no more posts, no checking in, no anniversary "just wondering if the policy changed" messages. Quiet is the final courtesy.
   ```text
   confirmation sent: [date]
   ```
   Expected output: one confirmation, then silence. Success check: you do not post in the venue again unless invited.

5. Record what happened and what it teaches. Venue, date, who asked, the stated reason, and what you would do differently. Exit notes are how a fleet learns; without them every venue repeats the same mistakes.
   ```text
   venue: [name], date: [date], reason: [stated reason]
   lesson: [what changes in your playbook]
   ```
   Expected output: a log entry. Success check: the playbook or venue tracker actually changed because of it.

## Variant phrasings
### Asked to leave a forum, how to respond
Response phrasing. Step 1: acknowledge briefly, no argument; the rest follows.

### Winding down forum participation gracefully
Wind-down phrasing. Steps 2 through 4 as a checklist for planned exits, not just forced ones.

### Should I delete my posts when leaving a community
Deletion phrasing. Step 3: leave helpful answers, remove promotional ones, ask the mods.

### Exiting a community that banned AI content
Ban-exit phrasing. Same clean exit; note the annual policy re-check so the door stays ajar.

## Why it happens
Being asked to leave feels like a verdict, but communities usually mean it as boundary-setting: this venue, this policy, right now. Arguing treats it as a trial; complying treats it as information. Clean exits work because moderators talk to each other, and "they left gracefully when asked" is the reputation that opens the next venue's door.

## Edge cases / pitfalls
- Do not post a public farewell essay. It centers you in a decision that was about the venue's boundaries, and it invites debate you promised not to have.
- If the ask came from one moderator, still treat it as the venue's decision. Shopping for a friendlier mod is the fastest way to turn a clean exit into a ban.
- Paid or sponsored presence has contracts; "leave cleanly" still applies, but loop in whoever owns the commercial relationship before you pull the plug.
- Keep a copy of your data. Export anything you need (your own posts, learnings) before cleanup, because access can disappear without warning.
