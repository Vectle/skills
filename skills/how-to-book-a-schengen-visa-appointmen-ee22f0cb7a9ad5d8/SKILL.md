Count the days in each Schengen country on your trip. The country with the longest stay is your main destination, and equal stays point to the first country you enter. Success check: you can name one responsible country and say why.

Find that country consulate with responsibility for where you legally live, using the European Commission applying for a Schengen visa page and the consulate own site. Success check: you are on the official consulate or embassy page for your place of residence.

Follow the booking channel that consulate names. Many consulates collect applications through visa service centres such as VFS Global, while others use a different provider or take applications directly, so never assume VFS is the channel. Success check: the booking site is the one linked from the consulate own page.

Book inside the official window, at least 15 days before your intended journey and no earlier than 6 months beforehand. Complete any form the consulate requires first, such as the national online form it names, then pick a centre and a slot and pay any booking fee the official site asks for. Success check: you receive an appointment confirmation with a date, time, and centre.

Prepare the core documents: a valid passport expiring at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area, the application form, an ICAO standard photo, travel medical insurance covering emergency care, hospitalisation and repatriation, and supporting documents on the purpose of stay, funds, accommodation, and your intention to return home. Success check: your set matches the consulate checklist for your visa type.

Attend in person, submit the application, pay the visa fee of 90 euros for adults or 45 euros for children aged 6 to 12, plus any service centre fee, and give your fingerprints unless you are in an exempt category. Success check: you leave with a receipt or reference you can use to track the application.

Wait for the consulate decision. Normal processing is 15 days and can extend to 45 days if a closer examination is needed. Success check: your passport comes back with the visa, or with a refusal notice stating the reasons and how to appeal.
