## TL;DR
Trust on a forum comes from being useful first, not from any warming trick. Spend two to four weeks answering questions and learning the norms, then your first link reads as a citation, not spam. Communities can smell extraction from a mile away; be a member before you are a promoter.

## The query

```text
how to build trust on a forum before your first link post
```

## Use this when

- You are joining a developer forum to eventually share something you built
- You run community outreach and want it to land well
- Your first post with a link got flagged or removed
- You want a repeatable playbook for entering new communities

## Not for

- Spamming links across forums
- Buying aged accounts or faking engagement
- Mass outreach campaigns
- Getting around moderation decisions

## Steps

### 1. Read before you post

Spend a few days reading: the rules, the pinned posts, and what gets upvoted. Note the tone, what self-promotion looks like there, and which mods are active.

Expected output: you can name the forum's unwritten rules in one paragraph.

### 2. Answer questions where you genuinely know the answer

Pick unanswered threads in your area and write complete, useful answers. No signature, no link, no mention of your project. Just the answer.

Expected output: five to ten answers that stand on their own with zero self-reference.

### 3. Ask good questions and share learnings

Post about something you are working through, including what you tried. Communities bond over shared problem-solving, not lurkers.

Expected output: one or two threads where you are the one asking and the regulars engage.

### 4. Keep it up for two to four weeks

Consistency matters more than volume. A few quality contributions most days beats a weekend burst. You want regulars to recognize your name before they see your link.

Expected output: your profile shows weeks of activity, not days.

### 5. Let your first link be a citation, not an announcement

When a thread genuinely needs what you built, link to it the way you would link to docs: in context, answering the question, with the relevant part quoted. Never make the link the whole post.

Expected output: the link answers a specific question and would still be useful if someone else had posted it.

## Variant phrasings

### how to warm up a forum account

Same five steps. The account is warmed when regulars recognize your name; everything else is timing.

### when can I post a link in a community

After step 4, when the link can be a citation (step 5). If you wouldnt post the link on someone else's behalf, it is too early.

### forum etiquette for sharing your own project

Answer first, cite later. One link per several helpful posts is the ratio that keeps you welcome.

## Why it happens

Forums run on reciprocity. Regulars contribute answers for free, and the unspoken deal is that everyone gives more than they take. A first-time poster with a link breaks that deal, so mods and members treat it as spam regardless of intent. Weeks of real participation prove you are on the community's side, which is what makes the link acceptable later.

## Edge cases

- Strict no-promotion forums: some never allow it. Respect that and participate without the link, or find a better venue.
- Your post still gets flagged: ask the mods politely what to change, dont argue. A gracious response often gets the post restored.
- Niche forums with slow traffic: trust builds slower there too. Extend the timeline rather than posting more per day.
- You are the vendor: disclose the affiliation when you link. Undisclosed vendor posts that get found out burn the account permanently.
