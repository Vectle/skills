Find the deadline first: open your confirmation email and read the cancellation policy line for your rate, or open the reservation and read its rate details. Success check: you have the exact date and time, in the hotel's local time, not a guess based on the brand.

Sign in to marriott.com or open the Marriott Bonvoy app and go to My Trips. Success check: the reservation opens with modify and cancel options.

Choose Cancel Reservation and confirm while you are still inside the window. Success check: you get a cancellation number and a confirmation email; without those, the room is not canceled.

If you booked with points or a nightly upgrade award, cancel inside the same window. Success check: the points or award are back in your account; outside the window they are forfeited or charged.

If the cancel option is not visible, do not assume you are stuck or that deleting the app cancels anything. Call Marriott reservations or the property directly. Success check: a person confirms the cancellation and gives you a cancellation number.

## Community-reported tips (not Marriott's published policy)

The tips below come from guest reports, hotel forums, and consumer guides, not from Marriott. Treat them as street knowledge that can drift, and check your confirmation's cancellation terms before you rely on one.

- Past or near the deadline, contact the property itself, not Marriott central reservations. Central cannot override the property's policy, but the property can waive its own fee, and firsthand reports show it repeatedly doing so, including a December 2025 case where a property waived a $223 charge past the deadline and the Free Night Award was re-deposited.
- Ask politely and briefly, as a courtesy, and do not lead with elite status. In the firsthand record, the respectful property ask is what worked; the one entitlement-framed case, run through central, was denied and the $371 fee upheld. Property discretion cuts both ways (one property refused a weather waiver outright), so treat this as often reported to work, never as a rate anyone can promise.
- If a flight cancellation is your reason, call the property the moment you know and offer proof, such as the airline's cancellation notice or a FlightAware screenshot. Multiple travelers report clean waivers on exactly that pattern.
- Elite status helps only as an unpublished courtesy at the property. It does not extend your deadline or create a waiver right. Mention your tier when you make the property ask; do not cite it as an entitlement.
- On a points or Free Night Award booking, a late cancel does not forfeit the points: they are re-deposited, and the penalty, if the property does not waive it, is a cash charge to the card on file, typically one night at the retail rate. Budget for the cash fee, not lost points. A Free Night Award's own expiry, separately, cannot be extended, so do not build a plan on that.
- Hang up and try the other door: central reservations and the property are genuinely different decision-makers, so a no from one is not a no from the other.
- Community-described, with a real terms risk: some travelers move a flexible-rate stay to a distant future date, then cancel inside the new date's free-cancellation window. Marriott's published policy is binding per rate and property, a property may treat the new date's policy or the original rate's terms as binding, and on prepaid or Advance Purchase rates a date move creates no refund right at all. If a property agrees to move your date, read the new confirmation's cancellation line before assuming you can now cancel free. Flexible rates only.
