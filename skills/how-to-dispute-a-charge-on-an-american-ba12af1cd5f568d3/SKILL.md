Run the quick checks first. Check whether an additional card member made the purchase, whether the merchant bills under a different name, and whether it is a subscription or trial you forgot. Success check: you can say the charge is genuinely wrong or unrecognised, not just unfamiliar.

Contact the merchant if it is a merchant error. Amex says that is often the fastest way to resolve an incorrect charge. Keep the date, name, and outcome. Success check: you have a written record of the attempt or the merchant refusal.

If the charge is pending, monitor it instead of filing. In Statements and Activity, find it under Pending Charges and choose Alert Me When Posted. Success check: you get notified when it posts and can dispute it then.

Open the dispute on the posted transaction. Go to Statements and Activity, click the transaction to view details, and choose Dispute or Inquire about this Charge, then follow the prompts and pick the reason that matches what actually happened. You can also call Customer Care. Success check: the case appears in the Inquiry and Dispute Center under Manage My Disputes.

Provide the documentation Amex asks for for your reason. Amex and its dispute guides ask for supporting documents such as a receipt, an order or cancellation confirmation, correspondence with the merchant, or a photo of a defective item, depending on the claim. Success check: the documents you uploaded match the reason you selected, not just the fact that you were charged.

Understand which stage your case is in. Amex merchant guidance says it first works directly with the card member and tries to resolve the case before reaching out to the merchant. If it needs more, the merchant gets either an Inquiry, where Amex asks for documentation before debiting the merchant, or an upfront Chargeback, where Amex may debit the merchant up front and the merchant can try to reverse it with documents. In both cases the merchant has 20 days to respond. Success check: you know whether Amex is still reviewing with you, waiting on the merchant, or has already decided.

Track it and do not pay the disputed part. Amex says it removes the disputed amount from your required payment due until the matter is resolved, and that most disputes resolve within a month, with complex cases taking longer. Check status in the Inquiry and Dispute Center under Manage My Disputes, in View or Cancel Open Disputes. Success check: the case shows as resolved and the amount is either permanently removed or returned to your balance with the outcome stated.
