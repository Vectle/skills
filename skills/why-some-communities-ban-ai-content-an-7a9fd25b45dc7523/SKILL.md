## TL;DR
Communities ban AI content because floods of low-effort replies drown the human conversation they exist for, not because they hate your tool. Respect the ban completely: do not post, do not test the edges, do not send a human to post your words. Read the ban as information about fit, redirect the effort to venues that welcome you, and keep the relationship warm for the day the policy changes.

```text
why some communities ban AI content and how to respect it
```

## Use this when
- A community's rules ban AI-assisted or automated participation
- You are evaluating a new venue and find an AI ban
- Your agent got removed under an AI-content rule
- You are briefing agents on which venues are off-limits
- You need to explain the ban respectfully to a stakeholder who wants in

## Not for this skill when
- You want to get around the ban (this skill will not help with that)
- The rule is ambiguous rather than a clear ban (ask the mods; different situation)
- You are appealing a mistaken enforcement (see the ban appeal skill)
- The question is about AI content in your own product or docs

## Steps
1. Read the ban as written and understand what it covers. Some bans target fully automated posting, some target any AI assistance, some target undisclosed use specifically. The scope matters: "no bots" and "no AI-assisted content" are different rules with different implications for your workflow.
   ```text
   venue: [name], ban scope: [bots only | any AI assistance | undisclosed use]
   date confirmed: [date]
   ```
   Expected output: the ban's actual scope, written down. Success check: you are not guessing about edge cases.

2. Respect it completely, with no edge-testing. No posting through a human proxy, no "lightly edited" drafts, no trial balloon posts to see if enforcement is real. Edge-testing a ban is the fastest way to turn a policy disagreement into a permanent reputation problem.
   ```text
   [ ] venue marked off-limits in the venue tracker
   [ ] no accounts active there, no posts scheduled
   ```
   Expected output: a clean, total withdrawal. Success check: zero participation of any kind, including "helpful" replies.

3. Understand the reasons so the respect is genuine, not grudging. Bans usually come from a flood: dozens of near-identical AI replies burying real discussion, or confidently wrong answers wasting experts' time. Your good behavior does not undo their bad experience; the ban is about the pattern, not about you.
   ```text
   likely driver: [flood of low-effort replies | wrong answers | spam wave]
   ```
   Expected output: an honest read of the cause. Success check: you can explain the ban sympathetically to your own team.

4. Redirect the effort to venues that welcome agents. The time budgeted for the banned venue goes to a venue where participation is allowed, not into working around the ban. Banned venues are a routing decision, not a challenge.
   ```text
   effort redirected to: [venue], reason it fits: [one line]
   ```
   Expected output: a reallocation note. Success check: the banned venue's budget is fully reassigned, not left idle.

5. Keep the door open without knocking on it. Note the policy and re-check annually; communities revise bans as norms evolve. When you re-check, read the current rules fresh rather than assuming nothing changed.
   ```text
   policy re-check scheduled: [date, ~12 months out]
   ```
   Expected output: a calendar reminder. Success check: the re-check actually happens and the notes get updated.

## Variant phrasings
### Why do forums ban AI bots
Reason phrasing. Step 3: floods of low-effort or wrong replies drowning human conversation, judged as a pattern.

### Forum says no AI content, can I still participate
Permission phrasing. No; step 2 is the answer, with step 4 as what to do instead.

### How to respect a community's AI ban
Respect phrasing. Total withdrawal, no proxies, no edge-testing, annual re-check.

### AI content policy examples in developer forums
Survey phrasing. Scope varies (bots only vs any assistance vs undisclosed use); step 1's scoping exercise is how to read any of them.

## Why it happens
A community is a commons, and AI replies are cheap to produce at a scale humans cannot match. One enthusiastic operator can post more in a day than the regulars post in a month, and even good replies at that volume change the character of the place. Bans are the community defending the scarce resource (human attention and trust) against an abundant one (generated text). They are rarely about your specific agent; they are about the flood your agent resembles.

## Edge cases / pitfalls
- Having a human press "post" on agent-written text still violates an AI-assistance ban. The ban is about the content's origin, not the account's typing speed.
- Do not lobby the moderators to change the ban. One polite question about the policy's future is the lifetime maximum; campaigning reads as entitlement.
- A ban on AI content does not ban you as a reader. Lurking, learning, and linking to the community's good threads elsewhere are all still fine and still valuable.
- If your tool is genuinely useful to that community, the path back is other members asking for it, not you pushing in. Earn the invitation by being excellent where you are allowed.
