Open the reservation on southwest.com or in the app and choose cancel. Success check: you are at least 10 minutes ahead of the original scheduled departure time.

If the fare is refundable, choose the refund to your original form of payment. Success check: the confirmation shows a refund of the ticket value rather than a credit.

For any other fare, complete the cancellation and take the flight credit. Success check: the credit appears under My Account in View Travel Funds.

Read the expiration on the credit before you plan around it. Success check: you know whether you hold a 6 month Basic credit or a 12 month Choice fare credit, counted from the booking and ticketing date.

To transfer a Transferable Flight Credit, go to My Account, then View Travel Funds, then Transfer to someone else, with the recipient's name and Rapid Rewards number. Success check: the full credit amount moves; partial transfers are not allowed and a transfer cannot be reversed.

## Community-reported tips (not Southwest Airlines's published policy)

The tips below come from traveler reports, frequent-flyer forums, and consumer guides, not from the airline. Treat them as street knowledge that can drift, and check the current policy before you rely on one.

- When the fare drops, do not cancel and rebook on Choice and above. Use Change in Manage Reservations to switch to the same flight at the lower price; the difference comes back as a flight credit, to your card on refundable tiers, and as redeposited points on award bookings. Basic cannot be changed at all, so there the only route is cancel for credit and rebook, or upgrade the fare first.
- Basic's 6-month expiry runs from the day you bought the ticket, and travel must be completed by that date. Cancel four months in and only about 2 months of usable credit remain; buy more than 6 months out and a later cancel can leave a credit that is already dead.
- Mixing payments poisons the whole credit: if you pay with an old credit or LUV voucher plus cash, a later cancellation returns one credit expiring on the earliest date among the funds you used, cash portion included.
- A non-transferable credit, including a Basic credit, can be made transferable: book a Choice Preferred or Choice Extra ticket in the original holder's name using the credit, then cancel. The value returns as a Transferable Flight Credit, movable once, in full, to another Rapid Rewards member. This does not extend the expiry.
- Community-reported, no guarantee: multiple travelers in mid-2026 reports say adding a small Southwest gift card (about $15 in the reports) to that refundable-tier booking, so part of the gift card is actually used, makes the pooled credit come back transferable with no expiration shown. It leans on gift cards not expiring, and Southwest could close it at any time.
- A LUV voucher is not a flight credit. LUV vouchers are goodwill, anyone can use one, but they cannot cover taxes and fees and they expire 6 to 12 months from issue. Flight credits from a cancellation do cover taxes and fees.
- If a credit has already expired, call with the original confirmation number. Agents reportedly grant a one-time extension of about 3 months, or convert the value to a LUV voucher, but only after the expiry date, and never as cash.
- Self-serve in the app or Manage Reservations is the normal channel, up to 10 minutes before departure. Save the phone for expired credits, splitting a reservation to cancel one passenger, and adding a missing Rapid Rewards number to a credit.
