Find the release rule for your specific restaurant: its Resy listing usually states it, in the form of reservations opening a set number of days in advance, and the furthest bookable date on the calendar confirms the window length. Success check: you can name the exact day and time your target date opens.

Work backwards: release day is your dinner date minus the window length, and release time is usually midnight in the restaurant's time zone unless the listing says otherwise.

The day before, log in to Resy, save a payment card to your account, and confirm your party size. Many rooms require a card to book, and account or card steps during the drop lose tables.

A few minutes before the release, open the restaurant page with your date and party size set. At the release second, refresh and take the best available time without deliberating; you can often adjust later, but you cannot un-sell a table.

Finish checkout until you see the confirmation screen, then verify the confirmation email arrived. A table sitting in your cart is not a booking until checkout completes.

If you miss out, set a Notify for that date immediately, since cancellations cluster near the free-cancellation deadline, and be ready at the same release time the next day if a nearby date works.

## Community-reported tips (not Resy's published policy)

The tips below come from diner reports and reservation guides, not from Resy. Treat them as street knowledge that can drift.

- Do not assume midnight. Guides that verify release rules venue by venue find most in demand rooms actually drop at 9 or 10 am local time; midnight drops exist but are the minority. The exact hour for your restaurant is usually stated in the Need to Know section of its Resy page.
- At a hot room, prime weekend slots are gone in 10 to 30 seconds. Decide on two or three acceptable times before the drop and take the first one that appears.
- Early week dates and the first or last seating of the night are consistently the easiest slots to land at the drop, if your plans can bend that way.
