Open the event on Ticketmaster, sign in, and look at the seat map and seat list. Success check: you can see whether any primary, Platinum, or resale inventory is showing at all.

Use the filters to show Verified Resale tickets and look for the pink circle icon on the seat map and the Verified Resale badge. Success check: the seats you see are marked as verified resale, which Ticketmaster says are verified and secure.

Compare any Official Platinum seats on price before buying. Platinum gives access to some of the most in demand seats at a price set by the event organizer, Ticketmaster says it does not use algorithmic surge pricing for them, and you get no package extras with them. Success check: you know whether you are paying a Platinum primary price or a fan set resale price.

If the quantity or section you want is not there, set the artist as a favorite, save the event to your watchlist in the app, and turn notifications on. Success check: you will get alerts instead of relying on manual checks alone.

Keep checking as the event gets closer, including the last days and the day of the show. Ticketmaster advises trying closer to the event in case more tickets are listed, and its last minute guide says to check the event listing for newly released inventory and resale listings, sometimes available up until showtime. Success check: you catch a new listing or a newly released block when it appears.

Check the venue box office as a parallel route, since some venues release additional tickets at the physical box office on the day of the show. Call the venue or check its site first. Success check: the venue confirms whether a day of show release exists for your event.

Buy as soon as a seat you accept appears, and complete checkout in that session. Success check: you get an order confirmation and the tickets appear in your account.
