## TL;DR
A good support apology has four parts: own it without hedging, say what happened in one sentence, fix it or say exactly when it will be fixed, and say what changes so it does not repeat. Skip the groveling and skip the excuses. Customers forgive mistakes; they do not forgive evasiveness.

## The query

```text
how to apologize for a support mistake
```

## Use this when

- Your team gave a customer wrong information
- An SLA was missed or a promise broken
- A support action caused a customer problem
- Writing apology macros or training agents

## Not for

- Legal liability situations (get legal involved)
- PR-level crises
- Apologizing for company policy the customer dislikes
- Mistakes the customer made (different playbook)

## Steps

### 1. Own it in the first sentence

"We got this wrong" or "I gave you the wrong information." No "we apologize for any inconvenience," no passive voice, no "mistakes were made." The first sentence sets the tone for everything after.

Expected output: an opening line with a clear subject and verb.

### 2. Say what happened, briefly

One or two sentences of fact. "I told you the refund would arrive Friday; it actually takes five business days, so it arrives Wednesday." Facts, not self-flagellation.

Expected output: the customer understands exactly what went wrong.

### 3. Fix it or commit to a fix with a date

Do the fix now if you can. If you cannot, say what happens next and when: "I have reissued the refund; it will arrive by Wednesday." A apology without a fix is just words.

Expected output: a completed fix or a dated commitment.

### 4. Say what changes

"This should not have happened, and here is what we changed" - the macro is updated, the team was briefed, the process now has a check. One sentence is enough. It turns a mistake into evidence of competence.

Expected output: one concrete preventive change.

## Template: the apology

```text
Hi [Name], we got this wrong, and I am sorry.

What happened: [one to two sentences of fact].

What I have done: [fix completed, or commitment with date].

What changes: [one concrete preventive step].

If anything about this still does not look right, reply here and I will sort it personally.
```

## Variant phrasings

### sorry we messed up support email

Steps 1 through 4 in order. Do not skip the fix.

### how to say sorry to a customer professionally

Step 1 plus step 4. Plain ownership and a preventive change read as professional; groveling does not.

### apology for giving wrong information

Steps 1 and 2 carry it. Name the wrong info and the right info explicitly.

## Why it works

Research and experience agree: customers judge the recovery more than the mistake. A fast, plain, complete apology with a fix often leaves the customer more loyal than if nothing had gone wrong. Hedged apologies do the opposite: they signal the company cares more about liability than the customer.

## Edge cases

- The mistake is still unfolding: apologize for what is known, commit to updates, do not speculate.
- Legal exposure: loop in legal before writing anything. Some apologies create liability.
- The agent is not at fault (product bug, another team): the team still owns it to the customer. Sort blame internally.
- Repeat mistakes to the same customer: acknowledge the pattern explicitly. "This is the second time" must come from you, not them.
